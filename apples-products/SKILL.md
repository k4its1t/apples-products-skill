# Apple’s Products

Create original English product headlines that feel simple, confident, human, and product-led. Learn from Apple’s high-level copy patterns without copying, closely paraphrasing, or presenting the result as Apple-authored.

## Before writing

Read [references/style-system.md](references/style-system.md). Read [references/official-pattern-sweep.md](references/official-pattern-sweep.md) when broad current-style calibration or product-family comparison would improve the result. Read [references/recent-product-generation-headlines.md](references/recent-product-generation-headlines.md) for iPhone, iPad, Mac, or AirPods work; successor or upgrade framing; family-role differentiation; or comparison with product generations from 2019–2025. Read [references/historical-product-headlines.md](references/historical-product-headlines.md) when the brief calls for a legacy or retro register, concerns a discontinued category, or needs a wider collision check against earlier Apple copy.

Ground every line in the brief. Identify:

- the product and placement;
- the audience and desired response;
- the single strongest differentiator;
- the human benefit it creates;
- any proof, constraint, required phrase, or prohibited claim.

If facts are thin, generate concept-level lines that do not imply missing specifications. Never invent benchmarks, awards, compatibility, health outcomes, or claims such as “best,” “first,” “fastest,” or “all-day.” Mark a useful proof gap rather than filling it with hype.

## Compose

Build each headline around one organizing idea. Prefer a concrete noun or active verb, a familiar phrase turned once, or a crisp contrast between technical capability and human payoff. Use one strong device per line; occasionally two when they reinforce each other.

Default to sentence case and compact punctuation. A hero line is usually 2–8 words; a feature line may be slightly longer when clarity needs it. A period can create confidence or rhythm, but should not become a mannerism.

Develop candidates across genuinely different routes:

- direct benefit;
- compact contrast or inversion;
- rhythmic fragments;
- product-specific wordplay;
- technical fact translated into felt experience;
- an update or reveal construction.

Discard lines that are generic enough for any product, require explanation, force a pun, stack empty adjectives, or sound like startup boilerplate. Avoid reflexive use of “reimagine,” “redefine,” “revolutionary,” “next level,” “the future of,” “game-changing,” “magical,” and “powerful.” Use these only when the idea and evidence make the word specific.

## Select and deliver

Score promising lines from 0–2 on each dimension:

1. truth and product fit;
2. immediate meaning;
3. brevity and spoken rhythm;
4. distinctive but effortless turn;
5. usefulness in the requested placement.

Keep lines scoring at least 8/10. Check that finalists do not echo an entry in the current, recent-generation, or historical sweep, or a famous Apple campaign. Reference corpora broaden the set of mechanisms; they are not permission to revive dated claims, period vocabulary, or signature slogans.

Unless the user specifies a format, return:

1. three strongest headlines, ranked;
2. five additional directions with real variation;
3. one short support line for the top choice;
4. a brief note for any fact or claim that still needs verification.

Do not expose brainstorming, rejected drafts, or the numeric scoring unless asked. Keep the response in English unless the user requests another language.

## Campaign mode

When asked for a page or campaign system, establish one verbal idea and vary its expression instead of repeating the same grammatical trick. Deliver a hero line, one support line, 4–8 feature-section headlines, and optional CTA labels. Make adjacent headings change pace while remaining recognizably related.

## Audit mode

When rewriting supplied copy, preserve its factual meaning and required terminology. Explain only the most consequential issues—usually generic language, excess length, weak benefit, forced wordplay, or unsupported claims—then provide improved options.
